Initial situation :
The production of lithium-ion batteries is rapidly scaling worldwide, driven
by demand for electric mobility and energy storage. New large-scale
manufacturing facilities, particularly in Europe, must meet high
requirements in terms of quality, efficiency, and cost. At the same time,
battery production is characterized by complex processes, sensitive
materials, and strict environmental conditions such as dry room operation.
Despite increasing automation, many process steps remain partially
manual, especially in cell assembly, material handling, logistics, and
quality assurance. These manual interventions limit scalability and
introduce variability. Robotic systems offer significant potential to improve
process stability, increase efficiency, enhance safety, and enable better
data integration for digitalization. However, challenges remain regarding
integration, handling of delicate materials, operation under dry room
conditions, and economic feasibility.
Master's thesis / Bachelor's thesis
Your task :
The objective of this work is to analyze and evaluate the use of robotic
systems along the battery cell production value chain. The study begins
with an overview of current manufacturing processes and their specific
requirements. Based on this, suitable application areas for robotics are
identified, including industrial robots, collaborative robots, and mobile
systems. Typical use cases include material handling, automated
inspection, flexible assembly, and intralogistics. These applications are
then assessed from a technical and economic perspective, considering
factors such as automation potential, cost, quality impact, and scalability.
Building on this, selected use cases are developed into concrete
application scenarios, taking into account different cell formats and
production environments. Finally, the work outlines future trends in
robotics and provides recommendations for industrial implementation and
further research.
Robotic Systems in Battery Manufacturing: Potentials,
Applications, and Implementation Strategies
