## 2. Registration and abstract

The registration abstract functions as a compact research proposal, not merely the final thesis abstract.

Include:

- German and English thesis titles
- Motivation and practical problem
- Objective
- Research gap or unresolved problem
- Clear research question
- Scientific approach/method
- Work packages/subtasks
- Desired results
- Proposed thesis structure

The checklist/template indicates approximately 1.5 pages: about one page for motivation, objectives, task, and research question, followed by about half a page of work packages/structure in bullet points.

Timing in the bundled guideline:

- Send the draft abstract to the supervisor about seven days before the registration date so supervisor and group-lead review can occur.
- After approval, the final PDF and signed registration form are due to the PEM colloquium process by 18:00 on the day before the registration date.
- Registration dates normally occur every two weeks on Thursday; confirm the actual date and faculty-specific process.
- The student is responsible for choosing the correct registration form, checking eligibility, obtaining/forwarding signatures, and submitting documents to the appropriate office.
- Faculty 4 and Faculty 6 processes differ. Confirm the applicable procedure with ZPA/supervisor.
- If work at PEM premises/eLab is expected, indicate this and complete the required safety acknowledgement/instruction.

## 3. Required scientific logic

Use this thesis logic:

```text
Motivation and practical deficit
-> state of the art/science and research deficit
-> requirements for an evaluation or solution approach
-> approach and methodology
-> validation/application
-> conclusion and outlook
```

For this thesis, translate it as:

```text
Battery-manufacturing problem
-> existing robotics/automation knowledge and evidence gap
-> process-to-robotics evaluation framework
-> transparent literature/case/expert method
-> selected use-case application scenarios and validation
-> implementation recommendations and future research
```

The thesis must contain a clear research question. A catalogue of robot applications alone is insufficient.

## 4. Literature and evidence rules

- Include foundational literature and recent specialist research.
- Focus on literature from roughly the last five years unless using recognized foundational work.
- Use an appropriate mix of German and international/English sources.
- Do not build the bibliography mainly from internet pages.
- Non-scientific web sources may provide context but should not carry central scientific claims.
- Bachelor, master, and diploma theses are not acceptable as core citable sources under the bundled PEM guidance.
- Study relevant PEM and RWTH publications before writing the thesis.
- Support key state-of-the-art statements with references.
- Record exact page numbers for citations whenever the source provides stable pages.
- Treat vendor claims about performance, savings, or maturity cautiously and identify them as vendor evidence.

For working notes, record evidence using:

| Claim | Source type | Source | Page/section | Direct evidence or inference | Thesis use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Never invent missing quantitative values. Record them as evidence gaps or possible interview questions.

## 5. Writing and thesis format

- Use the current official Word/LaTeX template.
- Use precise, concise scientific language.
- The bundled guideline gives approximately 80 pages plus appendices for a master's thesis; verify against the current examination regulations.
- Summary and outlook together should normally not exceed two pages.
- Define and use abbreviations consistently and maintain the required lists of symbols/abbreviations, figures, and tables.
- Explain every table and figure in the text and refer to it at least once.
- Keep tables, figures, captions, chapter numbering, headers, and page numbering consistent.
- Use the current PEM citation style/Citavi template or another style explicitly approved by the supervisor.

## 6. Figures and editable sources

- Create thesis figures in an editable format, preferably using the current PEM figure PowerPoint template or another approved vector-capable tool.
- Use the specified PEM visual style, including Arial and the template color/line conventions.
- Avoid screenshots, scanned figures, and low-resolution raster graphics.
- If adapting an external figure, redraw it where appropriate and cite the source/adaptation clearly.
- Keep the editable creation file (`.pptx`, Visio, etc.) as well as the inserted/exported version.
- Submit figure source files with the final thesis materials.
- Use meaningful captions, chapter-based numbering, and source notes.

## 7. Data and project organization

- Maintain a shared Sciebo/data-exchange folder with the supervisor, or an agreed alternative.
- Keep literature, protocols, interview/case records, data, analysis, citation databases, and figure sources sorted.
- Preserve enough provenance to reproduce tables, ratings, and conclusions.
- Suggested repository roles:
  - `literatures/`: source PDFs
  - weekly folders: learning notes and interim evidence
  - `docs/`: stable project requirements and methodology
  - future registration folder: approved abstract and forms
  - future thesis folder: manuscript, figures, tables, appendices, and final versions

## 8. Submission and archive checkpoints

According to the bundled guideline/checklist:

- Remind the supervisor about submission roughly two weeks before the deadline.
- Send a preliminary thesis version to the supervisor about two weeks before planned submission.
- Provide the supervisor with the final PDF and editable Word file; coordinate separately if using LaTeX.
- Provide all relevant working materials in the shared folder.
- Follow the faculty/ZPA requirements for physical copies; the bundled Faculty 4 checklist mentions two bound copies, but current applicability must be verified.
- Archive the digital thesis through the current PEM process. The bundled guideline asks for title, submission date, supervisor/group, six keywords, and any confidentiality/embargo note.
- Do not rely on email addresses or deadlines in this file without checking the current official process.

## 9. Colloquium

The bundled materials specify:

- Approximately 15 minutes presentation plus 10 minutes questions/discussion
- Roughly 10–15 slides, with about 1.5–2 minutes per content slide
- Presentation structure: motivation/problem -> research question -> method and main results -> conclusion and further research
- Send the presentation to the supervisor about one week before the colloquium for approval.
- The bundled process closes colloquium registration roughly one week beforehand and requires approved presentation/digital thesis materials by 18:00 on the preceding day. Verify exact current dates.

## 11. Project-specific research guardrails

- Core scope: lithium-ion battery manufacturing. Always state whether a claim or case concerns cell, module, or pack production.
- Scope update after the 2026-08-12 supervisor meeting: Thomas stated that pack end-of-line testing is an acceptable current direction. Candidate B in pack EoL/DCR testing may therefore be deepened as a provisional case. The registered title and the relationship to the original wording `battery cell production value chain` must still be made explicit in the registration abstract.
- Adjacent scope unless explicitly broadened: recycling, detailed control algorithms, and detailed electrochemical design. Humanoid robotics may be evaluated as a primary comparison object or future scenario only when tied to a concrete battery-manufacturing task, comparison baseline, evidence boundary, and credible evaluation/evidence strategy. Expert review, experiment, or simulation may strengthen that strategy but is not universally mandatory.
- Current research bridge: battery fundamentals -> process requirements -> robotics opportunity -> implementation strategy.
- Evaluate a robotic system as more than a robot arm: include end effector, vision/sensors, fixtures, safety, process-equipment interfaces, MES/traceability, material flow, and operating environment.
- Separate core process equipment from surrounding robot-suitable handling, transport, inspection, sampling, and data tasks.
- Do not finalize use-case rankings until the underlying process problem, evidence quality, technical constraints, and required economic data are explicit.
