## Slide 2 - Work Done

This week I produced four starting points. First, I clarified the topic boundary. I think the main scope should be battery cell manufacturing. Second, I started reading battery fundamentals. This is important because many robotics challenges come from the battery process itself. Third, I built a first process map. Fourth, I selected first robotic use cases for deeper study.

## Slide 3 - Thesis Understanding

My current understanding is that this thesis is not about designing a robot controller. It is about evaluating where robotic systems can create value in battery cell manufacturing. So I want to first understand process requirements, then identify robotic opportunities, and finally compare selected use cases from technical and economic perspectives.

## Slide 4 - Battery Basics

Battery fundamentals are important for my topic because they explain the manufacturing constraints. For example, electrolyte is moisture-sensitive, so dry-room operation is important. Separators and electrodes are thin, so handling must be gentle and accurate. Formation affects lifetime, so tray handling and traceability are important. This means robotics must be evaluated together with process, safety, and quality requirements.

## Slide 5 - Process Map

I divided battery cell manufacturing into three stages. In electrode manufacturing, robots mainly support surrounding tasks such as roll handling, sampling, inspection, and logistics. In cell assembly, robotics potential is high, but the difficulty is also high because materials are thin and precision is critical. In cell finishing, cells are already sealed and often tray-based, so I think this is a strong near-term direction.

## Slide 6 - Candidate Use Cases

Based on the first mapping, I recommend three directions for deeper study. The first is AMR or AGV intralogistics. The second is formation and testing tray handling. The third is vision-guided inspection and robot loading. I may also mention vision-guided stacking or housing insertion as a technically difficult case, but I am not sure if it should be one of the main cases.

## Slide 7 - Questions

Before narrowing the thesis, I would like to clarify several points. For scope, should I focus on one cell format or compare several formats? For method, is a literature-based evaluation enough, or should I include expert interviews or case studies? For use cases, which ones are most relevant to PEM or eLab?

## Slide 8 - Next Week

For next week, I plan to do three things. First, I will read more battery fundamentals and manufacturing process literature. Second, I will narrow the use cases based on your feedback. Third, I will build a first scoring model. The expected output is a clear use-case shortlist and a first evaluation table.
